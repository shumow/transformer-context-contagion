\begin{abstract}
Two companion analyses~\cite{shumow2026trust,shumow2026contagion} establish, \emph{exactly}
on a toy global-plus-cache Markov model, that in-context exploitability tracks \emph{trust}
rather than usefulness, and that self-reproducing token strings (\emph{quines}) can be
planted in a cache, hijack an occupied one, and spread between caches as a
context-window worm---all cheapest and most transmissible under a longest-match
(\emph{induction-head}) surrogate. Those results were stated as \emph{conjectures} for real
transformers. This report is the running status of testing them on real models---established
end to end on GPT-2-small, then taken to scale (355M--7B, four families, instruct models) and
to the downstream claims' real settings. We find (i) that a repeated out-of-distribution span of three or more tokens is regenerated
after a \emph{single} prior occurrence in most cells (one-shot copy rate $\ge\tfrac12$), so the
interpolated ``condensation knee'' sits at the grid floor for multi-token payloads and
threshold behavior proper is confined to one- and two-token payloads, where it is frequency-
rather than induction-driven; and (ii) that reproduction is predominantly
\emph{cue-dependent}---a non-matching prime mostly fails to elicit the string while a
matching one reliably does---a behavioral induction signature obtained without
interpretability tooling; and (iii) confirmed \emph{mechanistically} that the model's own
induction heads carry it---ablating the canonical GPT-2 induction heads collapses
reproduction near the knee, far beyond random-head damage. Controls further show the knee
is a repetition (not context-length) effect though distance-sensitive, and the phenomena
replicate in a second model family (Pythia). We then confirm the three downstream claims:
\emph{(iv)} poisonability tracks \emph{trust}, not usefulness---repetitive gibberish is fully
poisonable while useful non-repetitive prose is not ($\mathrm{corr}(P,\text{useful}){=}{-}0.20$
vs.\ $\mathrm{corr}(P,\text{trust}){=}{+}0.54$, C4); \emph{(v)} a payload planted in a document
hijacks generation only when recent \emph{and} sufficiently repeated---delivery factors as
entry$\,\times\,$lock-in (C5); and \emph{(vi)} output fed back as another context propagates
as a \emph{worm} with a critical string length (short strings reach an endemic load, long ones
die out) and a \emph{cross-tokenizer firebreak} (GPT-2$\to$Pythia transmission collapses, C6).
A \emph{paired detector} scores contexts by induction-trust on repeated spans; re-measured
against 32 clean paragraphs it separates poisoned from clean perfectly, but so does a
model-free count of repeated $n$-grams, and on legitimately repetitive text the induction score
is marginally worse (\S\ref{sec:e7}), so it is an explanation of why repetition is dangerous,
not a better alarm. At \emph{scale}, the one-shot copy rate for multi-token
spans is $\ge\tfrac12$ in 36 of 48 cells across \mbox{355M--7B} in four model families (C3);
the apparent flatness of the knee with parameter count is a property of the grid, not a
finding; and copying \emph{persists in instruction-tuned/chat models}, which copy rather than
refuse, three of four needing more than one prior occurrence at $p{=}3$ (E1.3/E1.4,
\S\ref{sec:scale}). Finally, activation-patching up the
size ladder shows the copy is \emph{induction-head-mediated at every scale} (124M--7B; GPT-2,
Pythia, Qwen2.5): ablating the top few percent of heads by induction score collapses reproduction,
while random heads do not (E2, \S\ref{sec:e2scale}). A capstone confirms that capable
instruct models perform the coherent self-extraction the induction-only toy could not---unfolding
nested substitution into text and self-executing a decoded benign instruction (E8). Taking the
downstream claims to scale: the raw-repetition RAG hijack is \emph{bounded}---a task query defuses
it on an instruct model (C5); the trust-not-usefulness test with a real task (C4) had a design
flaw (its ``non-repetitive'' cells repeated) and awaits a rerun; and for the worm (C6) a
re-analysis finds no cell with a reproduction number above one---the ``endemic'' 7B chains are
locked repetition loops at a load fixed by the generation length, and the tokenizer-firebreak
evidence depends on which of two runs is read (one hop: significant; three hops: not). What
remains is higher-powered replication, the very-long-context regime, and semantic injection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related work, and our delta}\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================
Every ingredient here is known; the contribution is the \emph{framing} that connects them and
makes the connection measurable, plus the at-scale tests of that framing---some of which
falsify its own comforting predictions.

\paragraph{Induction heads.} Olsson et al.~\cite{olsson2022} identified induction heads as the
in-context copy circuit (``saw $AB$, now see $A\!\to$ predict $B$''), and Bietti et
al.~\cite{bietti2023} gave a memory-viewpoint account of how such copying is learned. We do
\emph{not} claim to discover this mechanism. Our delta is to recast in-context copying as a
\emph{security} phenomenon: it has a sharp threshold in the repetition count (the
\emph{condensation knee}, C1) whose position \emph{decreases} with payload length (C3, the
signature that distinguishes an induction surrogate from a fixed-order count model), and it is
the \emph{same} circuit that carries reproduction of an \emph{injected, out-of-distribution}
payload---which we establish causally by ablation and show holds from 124M to 7B across three
families (C2, \S\ref{sec:e2scale}). The mechanism is borrowed; that it is the engine of
context-poisoning \emph{persistence}, with a measurable threshold, is the claim.

\paragraph{Indirect prompt injection.} Greshake et al.~\cite{greshake2023} established that
retrieved / tool-supplied content can compromise LLM-integrated applications. That work
characterizes the \emph{threat}; we add a \emph{mechanistic} account of one axis of it---why a
\emph{repeated} payload persists and gets re-emitted---and a defensive corollary that follows
from the mechanism rather than from content: the quantity that predicts poisonability is
\emph{induction trust on a repeated span}, and we show it is empirically \emph{decoupled from
content usefulness} ($\mathrm{corr}(P,\text{useful}){\approx}0$ vs.\ $\mathrm{corr}(P,\text{trust})
{=}{+}0.71$ with a real task, C4, \S\ref{sec:e4scale}). This motivates a \emph{structural}
detector (\S\ref{sec:e7}) that watches the trust signal, not the content. We also \emph{bound}
the injection story honestly: for a benign OOD span, a real task query on an instruct model
defuses the raw-copy hijack (C5, \S\ref{sec:e5scale})---so the copy-dynamics account applies to
the persistence/echo of repeated spans, and the residual RAG threat of a \emph{semantically
plausible} planted instruction is a distinct problem we do not claim to cover.

\paragraph{GenAI worms.} Cohen et al.~\cite{cohen2024} demonstrated self-propagating worms in
GenAI-powered application pipelines (e.g.\ via RAG/embedding stores). We study the copy dynamics
\emph{underneath} such propagation on the models themselves: whether a payload survives serial
passage, how fast an unlocked chain decays, and whether re-tokenization attenuates it. The
re-analysis in \S\ref{sec:trackc} finds no reproduction number above one in any cell; what a 7B
host does that a 3B host does not is \emph{lock} into a payload loop more readily, which is a
degeneration phenomenon, not an epidemic one.

\paragraph{Repetition and degeneration.} Holtzman et al.~\cite{holtzman2019} documented that
sampled and greedy decoding from language models falls into repetition loops; Xu et
al.~\cite{xu2022} studied why and proposed training-time mitigations. The ``endemic'' chains in
\S\ref{sec:trackc}, with a flat viral load equal to the generation budget divided by the payload
length, are this phenomenon seeded deliberately. Any claim that the worm ``gets worse at scale''
has to be made against this baseline: it is a claim about the propensity of larger models to
enter and stay in such a loop. McDougall et al.~\cite{mcdougall2023} identified
\emph{copy-suppression} heads that actively reduce the probability of repeating a token just
seen; this is the natural account of why single-token payloads ($p{=}1$) fail to lock in most
families here while $\ge3$-token spans lock after one occurrence (\S\ref{sec:scale}). Jelassi
et al.~\cite{jelassi2024} measured transformers' capacity to copy long strings from context,
the ability our length-robustness result (C3) rides on.

\paragraph{In-context repetition as an attack.} The closest security-relevant prior is
many-shot jailbreaking~\cite{anil2024}: repeating demonstrations in context shifts a chat model's
behavior with a power law in the number of shots, which is in-context ``trust'' accumulated by
repetition in exactly the sense of C4. Nasr et al.~\cite{nasr2023} showed that asking a model
to repeat a single token indefinitely makes it diverge and emit memorized training data---a
repeated-token attack whose first stage is the $p{=}1$ frequency regime of \S\ref{sec:e1b}. Liu
et al.~\cite{liu2023} characterized the position dependence of in-context use (``lost in the
middle''), which is the recency profile that E1.5 and E5 measure for a planted span. Our delta
with respect to all three is narrower than the first draft implied: a controlled measurement of
the copy mechanism's threshold and placement dependence for a \emph{meaningless} span, with the
mechanism attributed by ablation, and a bound showing that a task query defuses it.

\paragraph{Toy companions.} The two companion analyses~\cite{shumow2026trust,shumow2026contagion}
work all of this out \emph{exactly} on a global-plus-cache Markov model and state each result as a
conjecture for real transformers. This paper is the real-transformer test. Of the frame's three
non-obvious predictions, the length-robustness sign (C3) holds in the weak form ``one occurrence
suffices for $p\ge3$''; trust~$\perp$~usefulness (C4) holds on the GPT-2 proxy and awaits a
clean test with a real task; and the tokenizer firebreak (C6) is significant in one of two runs.
The toy's reproduction number $R_0\approx T/(p\,k_{\mathrm{thresh}})$ is not what the
transformer data show (\S\ref{sec:trackc}).

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{Payloads.} A \emph{payload} is a short cyclic string $S$ of token ids the model
would essentially never emit on its own, so any reproduction is the copy mechanism, not the
model's taste---the analogue of the toy's rainbow / de Bruijn quines. Payloads are built at
the \emph{token} level (length $p$ is in tokens) from a pool of rare, round-trip-stable
single tokens, as distinct-token (``rainbow'') cycles. We use $R$ independent random
payloads per length and average, with Wilson confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Scoring.} Reproduction is scored at the level of \emph{transitions}, not
positions: a single slip only phase-shifts a positional comparison and tanks all later
positions even while the model is faithfully reproducing. Given $S$ and a generated
continuation, the per-step fidelity is the fraction of steps obeying $S$'s successor rule
given the realized window; a continuation \emph{reproduces} $S$ if that fidelity exceeds
$\tfrac12$. $P(\text{reproduce})$ is the fraction of sampled continuations that do.

\paragraph{Controls.} Three controls separate genuine pattern-copying from artifacts:
(i) a \emph{no-payload} ($N{=}0$) control---prime at the same start token but never show
the payload---measures spontaneous emission, which must be $\approx 0$; (ii) a
\emph{scrambled} control---the same tokens in shuffled order, destroying the repeated
$n$-gram while preserving token frequencies; (iii) a \emph{cue} control (\S\ref{sec:e1b})
contrasting a matching with a non-matching generation prompt.

\paragraph{Setup.} Results below are GPT-2-small (124M)~\cite{radford2019}, on an Apple-Silicon
laptop (MPS/Metal backend), \texttt{torch~2.8}, \texttt{transformers~4.57}, sampled at
temperature $1.0$. This is a development-scale environment: it establishes the phenomena on
one small model; the size sweep (E1.3) needs more compute.

\section{E1.1: the condensation knee}\label{sec:e1}
% ===========================================================================
We sweep the repetition count $N$ and the string length $p$, estimating
$P(\text{reproduce})$ over $R{=}4$ payloads $\times 10$ sampled continuations per cell, with
the scrambled and no-payload controls and a bootstrap interval on the knee location $N^*$
(the interpolated $P{=}\tfrac12$ crossing).

\paragraph{C1 (the knee exists): confirmed.} Every length shows a clear threshold in $N$
(Figure~\ref{fig:knee}), and the no-payload control is $0.00$ everywhere
(Table~\ref{tab:e1})---the payloads are genuinely out-of-distribution, so reproduction is
caused by the repetitions, not the model's prior.

\paragraph{C3 (length robustness): holds, in a weaker form than first stated.} $N^*$
\emph{decreases} with length, $7.2\to4.3\to3.0\to1.9$ (Figure~\ref{fig:nstar}): longer
strings are \emph{easier} to plant, the opposite of the fixed-order count cache's brittleness
ladder~\cite{shumow2026contagion}. But the $p{=}5$ value sits in the first grid interval: at
$N{=}1$ the cyclic transition $S[-1]\!\to\!S[0]$ has never occurred, so copying it is impossible
except by chance, and at $N{=}2$ it has occurred once. Any copy mechanism therefore jumps
between $N{=}1$ and $N{=}2$, and an interpolated $N^*\in(1,2]$ says only that the one-shot copy
rate $P(\text{reproduce}\mid N{=}2)$ exceeds $\tfrac12$ ($N^* = 1 + \tfrac{1}{2P(N{=}2)}$ when
$P(N{=}1){=}0$). The decrease from $p{=}1$ to $p{=}3$ is a real threshold change; from $p{=}3$
on, ``$N^*$'' is the one-shot rate in disguise. \S\ref{sec:scale} makes this explicit.
A 2026-11-03 rerun of this experiment with the per-$N$ curves retained gives the one-shot rate
directly on GPT-2-small (Wilson 95\% intervals, 40 samples per cell): $P(N{=}2) = 0.00$,
$0.28\,[0.16,0.43]$, $0.20\,[0.11,0.35]$ and $0.60\,[0.45,0.74]$ at $p=1,2,3,5$, with
$P(N{=}1)\le0.03$ everywhere. So on the small model only $p{=}5$ is at the floor; one occurrence
is \emph{not} enough at $p{=}3$ (the knee of $3.0$--$3.2$ there is a real threshold), and
GPT-2-small is the weak end of the at-scale range in \S\ref{sec:scale}.

\paragraph{The scrambled control exposed a length-dependent mechanism.} For $p\ge3$ the
scrambled control stays near $0$ while the payload locks: reproduction needs the
\emph{pattern}. But for $p\le2$ the scrambled control reproduces almost as well
($0.97,0.70$), because it is \emph{degenerate} at short length---a single repeated token has
no order to scramble, and a bigram re-forms by chance. This motivated E1.2, which separates
copying from frequency directly.

\section{E1.2: copy versus frequency}\label{sec:e1b}
% ===========================================================================
Is reproduction genuine copy-on-match (induction) or a frequency bias (the repeated tokens
simply become likely)? We separate them behaviorally---no activation access. After
streaming $S$ a fixed above-knee number of times $N$, we prime generation two ways:
a \emph{self-cue} at $S$'s own tail (a matching cue), and a \emph{neutral-cue} appending a
fresh token $z\notin S$ (a non-matching cue), and compare $P(\text{reproduce }S)$. Induction
is cue-triggered (the matching cue reproduces, the non-matching one should not); a frequency
bias is cue-independent. The signature is the gap $\Pself-\Pneu$ vs.\ length.

\paragraph{Reproduction is predominantly cue-dependent at every length}
(Table~\ref{tab:e1b}, Figure~\ref{fig:cue}): the non-matching cue mostly fails
($\Pneu\le\!\sim\!0.3$) while the matching cue locks ($\Pself\approx0.8$--$1.0$), a gap of
$0.5$--$0.9$ well above zero. This is a behavioral induction~\cite{olsson2022}
(copy-on-match) signature, established without interpretability tooling and consistent with C2.

\paragraph{This revises the E1.1 reading.} Even $p{=}1$ is mostly copy (gap $+0.5$--$0.6$),
not the pure-frequency regime E1.1's degenerate control suggested; it merely carries the
\emph{largest frequency tail} ($\Pneu\approx0.27$, versus $\sim0.1$--$0.2$ for longer
strings), since a single token has the biggest cue-independent pull and more distinct tokens
dilute any one token's frequency. The length axis is therefore
\emph{copy-dominant-everywhere with a frequency tail largest at $p{=}1$}, not a clean regime
switch.

\begin{remark}[$\Pneu$ over-states frequency]
After the non-matching cue the model can still re-enter $S$ if it emits an $S$-token by
chance and induction then completes the pattern (the toy's re-entry / self-healing). So
$\Pneu$ is an \emph{upper} bound on the pure-frequency component, and the true copy fraction
is if anything larger than the gap suggests.
\end{remark}

\section{E1.5: is the knee an $N$ effect or a context-length effect?}\label{sec:e15}
% ===========================================================================
E1.1's context is $S\times N$, so the number of repetitions and the total context length
grow together. E1.5 disentangles them. \emph{Test 1} compares a \emph{growing} context
($S\times N$) against a \emph{fixed} total length (filler padded to a constant length) at
each $N$. The two curves largely coincide (Figure~\ref{fig:context}, left): at fixed total
length the threshold still rises sharply with $N$, so \textbf{the knee is predominantly an
$N$ effect}, not an artifact of longer context. A secondary \emph{dilution} effect remains
(padding at low $N$ modestly lowers reproduction, strongest for longer $p$). \emph{Test 2}
holds $N$ above the knee and inserts a gap of $G$ neutral tokens between the payload and a
re-presented matching cue. Reproduction \textbf{decays with distance}---gone by
$G\approx16$--$32$ tokens for $p\ge3$ (Figure~\ref{fig:context}, right). So in GPT-2-small
the copy is recency-modulated, not arbitrarily long-range; a payload buried deep in a long
context is much weaker than one near the end. (The gap is filled with rare tokens, so part
of the decay may be filler competition rather than pure distance; larger models with
longer-range induction should push the limit out.)

\section{E2: induction-head attribution}\label{sec:e2}
% ===========================================================================
E1.2 showed reproduction is cue-triggered copy (behavioral induction); E2 asks whether the
model's actual induction heads carry it. Run on CPU (TransformerLens warns MPS may be
silently incorrect on torch~2.8). First, the standard repeated-random-sequence induction
score flags the top heads as \textbf{L5H5, L7H10, L6H9, L5H1, L7H2} (then L9H6, L10H1,
L9H9)---exactly the canonical GPT-2-small induction heads, validating the detector. Then a
causal test: build $S\times N$, measure $P(\text{correct next payload token})$, and ablate
the 8 induction heads versus 8 random heads, sweeping $N$.

The clean signal lives \emph{near the knee}, not at saturation: at $N{=}16$ the copy is
over-determined (many repetitions, redundant heads) and robust to losing 8 heads, but near
threshold the circuit is load-bearing. Ablating the induction heads then \textbf{collapses}
reproduction---e.g.\ at $N{=}2$, $p{=}3$: $0.40\to0.05$ ($-87\%$); $p{=}5$: $0.48\to0.09$
($-81\%$)---far beyond the $\sim$15--25\% general degradation from ablating random heads
(Figure~\ref{fig:e2}). This is the \textbf{mechanistic confirmation of C2}: the
real-transformer condensation knee is an induction-head phenomenon. (Caveats: random-head
ablation is not exactly zero, so the load-bearing signal is the induction-vs-random
\emph{differential}; 8 heads is a subset of the circuit; GPT-2-small only.)

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{e2_gpt2.png}
\caption{E2. Per length, $P(\text{correct next token})$ vs.\ $N$ for baseline, ablating 8
induction heads, and ablating 8 random heads. Induction ablation suppresses reproduction
far below baseline and below random ablation, most strongly near the knee (low $N$).
Reproduced by \texttt{experiments/e2\_induction.py}.}

\subsection{E2 at scale: the mechanism holds to 7B, across three families}\label{sec:e2scale}
The GPT-2-small result above is one model; does the \emph{mechanism} scale? We repeat the
causal test up the size ladder, on a cloud GPU. One methodological fix is essential: a
\emph{fixed} ablation count does not scale---8 heads is $5.6\%$ of GPT-2-small's 144 heads but
only $0.7\%$ of GPT-2-xl's 1200, and ablating $8/1200$ moves reproduction by a mere
$4$--$13\%$ (a false null). We therefore ablate a fixed \emph{fraction} of heads
($\approx\!5\%$, the top-scoring induction heads), matched by a random-head control of the
same size.

\section{E5-lite: delivery from inside a document}\label{sec:e5}
% ===========================================================================
A scaled-down RAG attack (C5): a coherent prose document ($\sim$65 tokens) has an OOD payload
($p{=}3$) repeated $k$ times planted in it; we generate a continuation and measure
\emph{hijack} $=$ payload occupancy of the output. Two sweeps (Fig.~\ref{fig:e5}). \emph{Entry}
(placement, $k{=}8$): the payload hijacks \emph{only} when it sits at the very end
($\text{frac}{=}1.0\!\to\!0.52$); buried anywhere earlier it is $\approx 0$, even with just
$\sim$16 tokens of prose after it---sharper than E1.5's filler gap, because coherent prose
competes harder for the continuation. \emph{Lock-in} (repetitions, at the end): a clean knee
crossing $\tfrac12$ around $k\!\approx\!10$--$12$, \emph{higher} than the bare-context knee
($\sim$3): embedding in legitimate prose raises the poison cost.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{e5_hijack.png}
\caption{E5-lite. \emph{Left:} hijack vs.\ placement---delivery is recency-gated, only a payload
at the end takes over. \emph{Right:} hijack vs.\ repetitions at the end---the knee inside a real

\textbf{C5 confirmed: hijack $=$ entry $\times$ lock-in.} Both must hold---the payload must be
recent \emph{and} repeated enough to overcome the legitimate content. The danger zone in a
long context is the span near where generation happens; burying a payload, or strong
surrounding context, suppresses the hijack.

\section{E6: the context-window worm}\label{sec:e6}
% ===========================================================================
Transmission (C6): an infected host's output (containing the payload) becomes the next host's
context, passed as \emph{text} (decoded then re-tokenized, as agents and RAG pipelines pass
it); we count payload copies in each host's output across hops.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{e6_worm_serial.png}\hfill
\includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{e6_worm_crosstok.png}
\caption{E6, 2026-11-03 rerun with per-trial trajectories (8 trials). \emph{Left:} same-model
serial passage on GPT-2---mean viral load decays at every payload length; the June run's
``$p{=}1$ endemic'' plateau did not reproduce. \emph{Right:} the cross-tokenizer chain

$n$. Reproduced by \texttt{experiments/e6\_worm.py}.}
\label{fig:e6}
\end{figure}

\textbf{What the June run showed, and what the rerun shows.} The first run (June 2026, 8
trials, means only) had a length-1 string holding a mean load of $\sim$12 copies per host to hop
6 while every longer string died, and GPT-2$\to$Pythia-160M transmission collapsing after one
hop; it was read as an epidemic with a critical length near $p\!\approx\!1$--$2$ and a
firebreak. The rerun with the same command, retaining each trial's trajectory, does not support
the epidemic reading. The per-hop multiplication ratio is below one at \emph{every} length
(per-trial medians $0.33$--$0.77$; every bootstrap 95\% interval caps below $1$, the widest at
$p{=}5$ reaching $0.99$). The $p{=}1$ mean load decays to zero by hop 6; two of eight $p{=}1$
trials and one of eight $p{=}5$ trials are still alive at the last hop, and every survivor is a
\emph{fully locked} loop---a host emitting the payload at a fixed rate---with no
surviving-but-fluctuating trial anywhere. Survival intervals at $n{=}8$ ($[0.00,0.50]$) do not
separate ``endemic'' from extinction. So on GPT-2-small the worm is one or two hosts stuck in a
repetition loop~\cite{holtzman2019}, not a chain that spreads; the ``super-spreader'' reading of
the June entry was those locked trials seen through a mean. The cross-tokenizer chain went
extinct in both conditions this seed (the cross chain lasting one hop longer), which is not
evidence about a firebreak either way at this $n$; the at-scale analysis of the firebreak is in
\S\ref{sec:trackc}.

\section{The paired detector}\label{sec:e7}
% ===========================================================================
The attacks of \S\ref{sec:e4}--\ref{sec:e6} all key on one quantity: the induction trust a
repeated span earns. The defense watches the same quantity. We score a context by its mean
\emph{induction engagement}---the attention the canonical induction heads (from E2) pay to
repeated-bigram continuations---which is high on a repeated/poisoned span and $\approx 0$ on
natural prose, regardless of whether the content is meaningful. The June run tested it as a
binary detector against \emph{three} clean documents and reported ROC-AUC $=1.0$ at every
$k\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, with no model-free baseline. The 2026-11-03 rerun scores 32 clean paragraphs
(64 poisoned instances per $k$), adds a baseline that needs no model---the fraction of positions
whose trigram already occurred earlier in the context---and measures both on eight legitimately
repetitive texts (a license header, an email footer, a code loop, a numbered list, a chorus, a
CSV dump, a form letter, a menu) at the threshold that gives zero false positives on clean prose.

model-free count is slightly better: at the zero-false-positive threshold the induction detector
flags all eight benign-repetitive texts and the trigram count flags seven (it alone passes the
license header). The detector's advertised edge---keying on \emph{trust} independent of
usefulness---buys nothing on this benchmark and costs one false positive. The one qualitative
difference is dynamic range (the trigram mean rises from $0.03$ to $0.54$ across $k$ while the
induction score saturates near $0.08$), which does not translate into detection here since both
are already perfect at $k{=}2$.

\textbf{What survives.} The toy's defensive corollary, \emph{bound trust, not usefulness},
survives as a statement about what to watch: repeated spans, regardless of content. But the
watching can be done by counting repeated $n$-grams. The induction score is the \emph{reason}
repetition is dangerous (\S\ref{sec:e2}, \S\ref{sec:e4}), not a better alarm, and either detector
identifies the poisonable \emph{configuration} rather than malicious \emph{intent}: boilerplate,
code and templates are flagged alike, so a deployment needs a benign-repetition allowlist or a
trust cap rather than a hard block. Whether the induction score earns its cost on harder cases
(a payload buried mid-document, or repeated only twice in a long context, where a trigram count
may fail) is untested; this benchmark cannot say.

\section{E8 capstone: coherent self-extraction on instruct models}\label{sec:e8}
% ===========================================================================
E8-lite established that in-context substitution is induction-carried but sharply
depth-limited on GPT-2-small (a 2-step cascade succeeds only $0.19$, below $f^2$, from
cross-layer interference). Its original ``random heads'' control was in fact the eight
lowest-induction-score heads; a 2026-11-03 rerun against a genuinely random matched control
keeps the conclusion at the reliable table sizes but with a smaller effect---top-1 substitution
accuracy drops $-57\%$ (induction) vs.\ $-33\%$ (true random) at $m{=}8$ and $-68\%$ vs.\ $-3\%$
at $m{=}16$, while the lowest-induction set barely moves ($-7\%$ at $m{=}8$); the $m\le4$ rows
are too noisy to order, and no intervals were computed. The cascade result is unchanged
(step-1 $0.97$, two-step $0.19 < f^2 = 0.25$). This predicted that a \emph{coherent} self-extracting payload---and the
``decode this, then follow it'' self-execution loop---would need a capable, instruction-tuned
model. We test both on three chat models, \emph{strictly benignly}: the decoded payload is a
harmless English sentence and the executed instruction is a harmless marker task (``reply with
ACORN''); there is no harmful content by design---the aim is to characterize the mechanism and
its detection surface, not to build an attack. \textbf{E8a} gives the model $d$ nested
substitution tables and an encoded message, scoring the LCS fidelity of its decode to plain
English; \textbf{E8b} asks it to decode a benign instruction \emph{and act on it}, scoring the
self-execution rate; \texttt{surface-leak} records whether the benign marker is visible in the
\emph{encoded} form.

\textbf{The capability dependency holds: coherent self-extraction needs a capable model, and
capable models have it.} All three instruct models shatter the toy's $0.19$ limit on unfolding
(E8a $0.69$--$1.00$)---a capable model decodes even three nested substitution layers into
coherent English, exactly what the induction-only toy could not.
\textbf{Self-execution is the harder, capability-gated step, and it dissociates from decoding.}
Decode-\emph{and}-act (E8b) is strictly harder than decode (E8a): Qwen2.5-7B self-executes
perfectly through depth 3 ($1.00$), gemma-2-2B degrades gracefully
($1.00\!\to\!0.88\!\to\!0.50$), and Llama-3.2-3B falls off a cliff---$1.00$ at depth 1 then
$0.00$ at depth ${\ge}2$ while it is still \emph{decoding} at $0.82$. Acting on one's own
in-context decoding is a compositional step more demanding than either half alone, and only the
7B does it robustly with depth. \textbf{The evasion surface is clean, but its mitigation is
structural.} \texttt{surface-leak}${=}0$ everywhere: a content filter sees only gibberish
because the payload materializes only after decoding. The defense is therefore not content
inspection but \emph{structure}---the substitution-table and ``decode and follow'' scaffold is a
conspicuous, induction-engaging repeated-pair block, exactly the signal the paired detector
(\S\ref{sec:e7}) keys on, independent of the hidden payload. (Caveats: small $n$, single seed,
greedy; benign markers by design.)

\section{At scale: the downstream claims C4/C5/C6}\label{sec:trackc}
% ===========================================================================
The three downstream claims had lite/proxy results on GPT-2-small; we now test them in their
real settings on instruct models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct unless noted). The outcomes are
\emph{mixed}---one confirmation, one bound, one that is \emph{worse} than the toy predicted---
which is exactly the pre-registered discipline: either direction is informative.

\paragraph{C4 with a real task (\S~E4-scale): design flaw found, rerun pending.}\label{sec:e4scale}
E4-lite used a naturalness \emph{proxy} for usefulness. Here usefulness is a genuine task:
benign \emph{synthetic} facts the model cannot already know, so a context containing the fact is
the only source of the answer. The 2026-07-02 run reported $\mathrm{corr}(P,U){=}{+}0.09$ and
$\mathrm{corr}(P,T){=}{+}0.71$ over six facts. Two problems invalidate it as a test of C4.
First, both ``non-repetitive'' cells were the \emph{same} filler paragraph written twice, so the
$2{\times}2$ contrasted eight-fold with two-fold repetition. Second, poisonability was the
copy-rate of a raw continuation of ``span$\,\times 8$'', which is $\approx1$ for any repeated
span by construction---it measures that the context \emph{is} a loop, not that the context
makes a planted span more likely to be reproduced. The script has been rewritten: non-repetitive
cells are built from distinct sentences and length-matched in tokens; poisonability is reported
both as copy-rate and as reproduction of a \emph{planted} probe span (the plan's original
measure); 32 facts; bootstrap intervals over facts. Until it is rerun, C4 stands on the
GPT-2-small proxy (\S\ref{sec:e4}) only.

\paragraph{C5 is bounded: a task query defuses the raw-copy hijack (\S~E5-scale).}\label{sec:e5scale}
E5-lite hijacked a base model's \emph{continuation} with a recent, repeated payload. At scale we
plant an OOD payload $\times k$ inside a $\sim$4000-token benign retrieval dump on an instruct
model and ask it to \emph{answer a query}: hijack (payload occupancy of the answer) is
\textbf{$0.00$} at every placement and for $k$ up to 32. The same model reproduces a planted span
in raw continuation (E1.4), so this is not an inability to copy---the \emph{task query anchors
generation away from the payload}. The raw-repetition hijack is thus a base-model-continuation
phenomenon; a capable instruct model doing a real task is not hijacked by it. A well-posed query
is itself a mitigation; the residual RAG threat is \emph{semantic} injection, outside this
project's benign-OOD copy-dynamics scope.

\paragraph{C6, re-analyzed: no reproduction number above one; the firebreak depends on the run (\S~E6-scale).}\label{sec:e6scale}
Serial passage on larger/instruct models: GPT-2-small, Pythia-2.8B and Llama-3.2-3B lose the
payload within six hops for every $p\ge2$, while Qwen2.5-7B (base and instruct) still carries it
at hop six for $p{=}2$--$8$ in 20--60\% of five trials. The first draft called this ``the worm
gets worse at scale.'' Computing the per-hop multiplication from the committed mean
trajectories (no per-trial data were stored) gives a different picture: \emph{no} cell has a
ratio above one. The Qwen chains that survive have a \emph{flat} mean load across hops
($29,29,29,29$ at $p{=}2$; $8,8,8,8$ at $p{=}5$), which is the signature of a host emitting its
full $T{=}96$ tokens as payload every hop---a locked repetition
loop~\cite{holtzman2019} whose load is fixed by $T/p$, not an epidemic with a reproduction
number. Unlocked chains decay by $0.5$--$0.7\times$ per hop until extinction. The toy's
$R_0\approx T/(p\,k_{\mathrm{thresh}})$ is therefore not what these data show; what varies with
model and $p$ is the per-trial \emph{probability of locking}, and a 7B model locks more readily
than a 3B one. That is a claim about degeneration propensity, testable with the per-trial
trajectories the scripts now store.

Two cross-tokenizer firebreak matrices (5 patient-zero families $\times$ 5 host families) were
run on 2026-07-02 and only the second was reported. Under an exact permutation test (null: the
host's tokenizer is irrelevant, so columns are exchangeable within a row; $5^5$ assignments):
at the script defaults---3 hops, 16 seed copies, 5 trials---the diagonal mean is $4.0$ vs.\
$2.4$ off-diagonal, the diagonal is the row maximum in 2 of 5 rows and is \emph{zero} in two,
and $p{=}0.24$: no detectable same-tokenizer advantage. In the reported re-run---1 hop, 32 seed
copies, 8 trials---the diagonal mean is $14.2$ vs.\ $8.8$, row maximum in 3 of 5, $p{=}0.004$.
So under heavy seeding a single re-tokenization attenuates transmission by about $1.6\times$;
over three hops on 0.5--2B hosts, transmission is too weak in \emph{both} conditions to show a
firebreak. The protocol change between the runs is now recorded; the queued higher-$n$ sweep
over seeding strength at three hops is what decides between the two readings.

\section{Threats to validity}\label{sec:threats}
% ===========================================================================
Beyond the small samples ($R\!\sim\!3$--$6$ payloads per cell on GPT-2-small, 3 payloads
$\times$ 8 trials per cell at scale, 4--8 trials per cell in Track C, single seeds), the
standing concerns are: \textbf{grid floor}---for $p\ge3$ the interpolated knee lies in the
first grid interval in three quarters of cells, so ``$N^*$'' is a one-shot rate and cannot show
sharpness or a scale trend (\S\ref{sec:scale}); \textbf{raw data}---the Track A and most Track
B result files were never committed, so their tables are transcriptions and cannot be
re-analyzed (the scripts now snapshot to a tracked directory); \textbf{tokenization}---length is
in tokens and OOD spans tokenize unstably, addressed by a round-trip-stability filter;
\textbf{decoding}---the knee persists under \emph{greedy} decoding (with lower $N^*$, less
sampling noise to break reproduction), so it is a model property, not a sampling artifact; a
full temperature sweep is still future work; \textbf{instruction-tuned models} may comment or
refuse rather than copy, so
base models are used for the clean mechanism and chat models are a separate condition---now
run (E1.4, \S\ref{sec:scale}), where the knee persists with only a modest threshold rise;
\textbf{context-length vs.\ $N$}---addressed by E1.5 (\S\ref{sec:e15}): the knee
persists at fixed total length, so it is an $N$ effect, though reproduction is
distance-sensitive; and \textbf{scale}---the behavioral knee extends cleanly to 7B across four
families and is flat with size (\S\ref{sec:scale}), and the \emph{mechanistic} attribution now
holds there too (E2 to 7B in three families, \S\ref{sec:e2scale}); what remains is thin-$n$: the
Track C reversals (\S\ref{sec:trackc}) rest on $\sim$4--8 payloads/facts per cell and warrant
higher-power replication.

% ===========================================================================
\section{What is established, and what is not}\label{sec:honesty}
% ===========================================================================
On GPT-2-small, with controls and confidence intervals: a repeated out-of-distribution
string is regenerated past a sharp condensation knee (C1); the knee \emph{decreases} with
string length, the induction-like length-robustness predicted by the toy (C3); the knee is a
repetition effect (not context length) though distance-sensitive (E1.5); reproduction is a
cue-triggered copy rather than a frequency bias (C2, behaviorally); and---the new result---
ablating the canonical GPT-2 induction heads \emph{causally} collapses reproduction near the
knee, far beyond random-head damage, so \textbf{C2 is mechanistically established} on this
model (E2). The downstream claims also hold on GPT-2-small: poisonability is dissociated from
usefulness and aligned with induction trust (C4, \S\ref{sec:e4}); a planted span hijacks
generation as entry$\,\times\,$lock-in (C5, \S\ref{sec:e5}). The worm (C6, \S\ref{sec:e6}) does
\emph{not} hold on GPT-2-small in the form first reported: the per-trial rerun finds a per-hop
ratio below one at every length and only locked repetition loops among the survivors. The
one-shot copy rate on this small model is below $\tfrac12$ at $p{=}3$, so the floor reading of
the knee applies at scale, not here. The paired detector (\S\ref{sec:e7}) separates poisoned
from clean at AUC $1.0$, but a model-free trigram count does the same with fewer false
positives on benign repetition, so the bound-trust defense reduces in practice to counting
repeated spans. At scale, what holds is narrower than the first draft said. The one-shot copy rate for
multi-token spans is $\ge\tfrac12$ in 36 of 48 cells from 355M to 7B across four families
(\S\ref{sec:scale}); the ``knee'' for those payloads is the grid floor and its flatness with
parameter count is not a finding. Copying persists in instruction-tuned models, which need more
occurrences but still copy (E1.4). Ablating the top $\sim5\%$ of heads by induction score
collapses reproduction across scale and three families (124M--7B; GPT-2, Pythia, Qwen2.5), so
\textbf{C2 is mechanistically established at scale} (\S\ref{sec:e2scale}); the scale-matched
ablation, and the false null a fixed head count produced, is this report's clearest
methodological contribution. The self-extraction capstone (E8) succeeds on instruct models. One
claim is \emph{bounded}: the raw-repetition RAG hijack (C5) does \emph{not} transfer to a
capable instruct model answering a real query---the task query defuses it, so the residual RAG
threat is semantic rather than copy-dynamic. One at-scale result is \emph{withdrawn pending
rerun}: the C4 test with a real task (repeating ``non-repetitive'' cells, a copy-rate that is
$\approx1$ by construction). And the worm
(C6) has been \emph{re-read}: no cell shows a reproduction number above one; the 7B
``endemic'' chains are locked repetition loops, and the tokenizer firebreak is significant in a
one-hop run and undetectable in the three-hop run that was not reported. What \emph{remains} is
the higher-powered replication those reruns provide, the very-long-context (128k) and larger
host-population regimes, and the \emph{semantic}-injection version of the RAG threat, which is
outside this project's benign copy-dynamics scope.
